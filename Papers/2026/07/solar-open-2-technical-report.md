# Solar Open 2 Technical Report

## TL;DR
Solar Open 2 is a 250B-A15B MoE model built as a Korean sovereign AI for long-horizon agents, reaching a 1M-token context via a hybrid attention stack (1 softmax layer per 3 linear layers, NoPE, negative-eigenvalue KDA). It is trained efficiently through selective weight transfer from its predecessor and consolidates twelve domain specialists into one model via Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD), matching much larger models on Korean officework agent tasks.

## Abstract
> We present Solar Open 2, a 250B-A15B Mixture-of-Experts language model built for long-horizon agentic tasks, scaled up from Solar Open 1 (Solar Open 100B). To hold entire agent trajectories in a single context, Solar Open 2 reaches a 1M-token window through a hybrid attention stack that interleaves one softmax layer among every three linear-attention layers, using no positional encoding and a gated delta rule extended to negative eigenvalues. To train at this scale under a fixed compute budget, we make training efficient in two ways: a stronger starting point, and higher-value data. For the starting point, we initialize Solar Open 2 from Solar Open 1, transferring the 5.69B-parameter shared skeleton that survives the architectural change and learning everything else through full pre-training. For the data, we curate for value per token: quality- and rarity-aware data curation and mixture-ratio optimization refine a 20T pool into a 10T mixture that, at equal token budget, outperforms the Solar Open 1 recipe. To build its agent skills, we train twelve domain specialists across purpose-built scenarios, then consolidate them into a single model by Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD). Against comparably sized open-weight models on English benchmarks, Solar Open 2 leads on MMLU-Pro, LiveCodeBench, and the APEX-Agents agentic suite, and stays competitive with the strongest (DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiMo-V2.5) elsewhere. On Korean benchmarks, Solar Open 2 records the highest average of any model compared, including fast-tier closed APIs, and on Ko-GDPval, an in-house Korean officework-agent benchmark, it is competitive with DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T) at less than a sixth of its size.

## Highlights
- Hybrid attention stack (12 softmax / 36 linear layers, NoPE, negative eigenvalues) extends context from 128K to 1M tokens at ~1/4 the memory and compute of an all-softmax design.
- On a 10B-A1B proxy, the hybrid+NoPE architecture reached MMLU 0.55 in 210B tokens vs 671B for the GQA-only baseline (3.2x) and HellaSwag 0.63 in 210B vs 377B (1.8x).
- Selective weight transfer reuses only 5.69B params (2.3%) from Solar Open 1 — embeddings, norms, softmax q/k/v/o, remapped linear q/o, shared expert — reaching CE 1.8 in 12.6B tokens vs 21.5B from scratch (~1.7x fewer).
- Pre-training curated a 20T pool down to 10T with 4:6 real-to-synthetic, ≥15% each math/code, >80% English; equal-budget ablation showed gains led by en_code (+0.216) and ko_math (+0.204).
- Twelve specialists consolidated via MOPD using KL-only, full-vocabulary, on-policy distillation at one rollout per prompt (no reward term), cutting the rollout bottleneck by factor G.
- On Ko-GDPval (Korean officework agent), Solar Open 2 scored 86.8, essentially matching DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T, 86.9) at <1/6 its size, vs its predecessor's 3.4.
- On English benchmarks it leads on MMLU-Pro (86.2), LiveCodeBench v6 (92.4), and APEX-Agents (16.6), while trailing on SWE-Bench Verified (70.4 vs 73-74) and Terminal Bench Hard (28.3 vs 41.7).

## Method
Architecture: 48-layer sparse MoE (250B total, 15B active), 320 routed + 1 shared expert, repeating an S-L-L-L block (softmax-first, unlike Kimi/Qwen linear-first) 12 times. Softmax layers use GQA (64Q/8KV) with an elementwise sigmoid output gate; linear layers use KDA extended with negative eigenvalues (beta=2*sigma in (0,2), eigenvalue range [-1,1]) enabling state tracking/self-correction. No positional encoding — order carried solely by the recurrent linear state, removing RoPE extrapolation limits. Initialization via selective weight transfer: transplant shape-compatible weights from Solar Open 1 (identical 196,608 vocab enables embedding transfer), randomly init the incompatible parts (mainly the 320 routed experts, 241.7B). Pre-training is 4 stages: (1) selective transfer, (2) 10T general pre-training on a quality/rarity-curated mixture, (3) 1T intensive pre-training on higher-quality subset, (4) 0.9T length expansion to 1M with 4-checkpoint merging (final avg metric 0.745). Post-training: SFT -> multi-domain verifiable-reward RL -> train 12 specialists -> MOPD consolidation. Agent scenarios are synthesized (not harvested): general conversational (MCP/tool + policy-bound dialogue with verifier-first CUD tasks), coding (SWE across 5 scaffolds, terminal, front-end artifact with VLM-judge), and officework via OfficeVerse (11 industries x 12 task types, grounded in real public data producing xlsx/docx/pptx/pdf deliverables). RL infra is fully asynchronous: disaggregated trainer/actor with in-flight weight updates, fresh-token-fraction gateway for staleness control, length/staleness-aware batch sampling, direct double-sided importance sampling (token-level, GRPO-style), and environment-failure filtering with group-size repair. MOPD ships teacher pre-lm_head hidden states [L×H] (48x smaller than [L×V]) and reconstructs logits in 1,024-token tiles to avoid the full 196,608-vocab blow-up; one GPU-resident teacher plus 12 CPU snapshots swapped on demand with teacher-packed microbatches.

## Evals & Results
Baselines are open-weight models under 320B (DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B, MiMo-V2.5 310B, Command A+ 218B, Mistral Medium 3.5 128B) plus Solar Open 1, and for Korean two fast-tier closed APIs (Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.4 mini); all via an internal harness. English: leads on MMLU-Pro (86.2) and LiveCodeBench (92.4), best APEX-Agents (16.6), ties IFBench (80.0), second to DeepSeek on GPQA/HLE/HMMT/Multi-Challenge; gaps concentrate in SWE-Bench Verified (70.4) and Terminal Bench Hard (28.3). Korean: highest average of all six models (85.4 vs DeepSeek 84.9, GPT-5.4 mini 80.8, Haiku 69.6), leads Ko-GDPval (86.8), CLIcK, KBank-MMLU, KBL. Generational jump over Solar Open 1: +18.4 avg Korean, SWE-Bench 15.4->70.4, GPQA 66.2->86.3, Ko-GDPval 3.4->86.8. Ko-GDPval (170 tasks, 58 occupations) breakdown shows Solar Open 2 second only to DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T), weakest on the Quality dimension (79.2 vs 84.2). A blind pairwise Korean-conversation eval (835 turns) favored Solar Open 2 44.1% vs 26.6%.

## So What (for practitioners)
Hybrid linear/softmax stacks with NoPE plus negative-eigenvalue linear attention are a viable route to 1M-context serving at ~1/4 memory/compute — relevant if you hold entire agent trajectories in context. Tokenizer efficiency compounds in long agent runs: a language-tuned tokenizer (4.41 vs ~3.54 bytes/token on Korean) directly lowers inference cost and lengthens effective context. Cross-generation selective weight transfer (reusing only shape-compatible ~2.3% of weights) is a cheaper warm start than from-scratch when the backbone is preserved. For agent training, synthesizing verifiers first (execute mutation, record state, generate read-back tests) and training across multiple scaffolds prevents tool-schema overfitting. Fully asynchronous RL with per-token staleness masking (fresh-token gateway) plus length/staleness-aware batching salvages long-tail rollouts and avoids length bias. MOPD's KL-only, full-vocab, one-rollout-per-prompt recipe is a sample-efficient way to consolidate many specialists into one deployable model without reward-hacking surface — and the hidden-state-shipping trick makes full-vocab distillation tractable at 250B.

## Open Questions / Critiques
All headline numbers come from the authors' internal evaluation harness with no external replication, so cross-model comparisons should be treated cautiously. The 1M-context and 'unbounded length' claims rest on architectural arguments and small-proxy ablations rather than demonstrated long-context retrieval/recall benchmarks at full scale. Key agent gains (Ko-GDPval, OfficeVerse) rely on in-house benchmarks and synthetic data pipelines heavily graded by LLM/VLM judges, raising judge-bias and contamination concerns despite claimed decontamination. Acknowledged weaknesses remain in repo/terminal-level SWE and fine-grained numerical precision in deliverables. The paper omits data compositions for SFT and multi-domain RL, and the arXiv ID/dates (2026/2607) appear anomalous, so provenance and reproducibility are limited.
